Divorce & Matrimonial Lawyers in Delhi and Gurgaon

Our matrimonial practice covers mutual and contested divorce, alimony, child custody, domestic violence, maintenance petitions, and criminal complaints arising from marital disputes. As a divorce lawyer in Gurgaon and Delhi, we draft settlement agreements, appear in the family courts across Delhi and Haryana, and pursue negotiated outcomes where appropriate.

Matrimonial proceedings are as much about timing and documentation as about argument. Where a mutual settlement is achievable, it is usually faster and less costly than a contested trial; where it is not, the petition must be built carefully from the first pleading, because what is stated at the outset governs what can be proved later.

  • Maintenance and alimony petitions, interim and permanent
  • Representation in domestic violence and Section 498A matters
  • Negotiation and drafting of separation and settlement agreements

Bail & Criminal Defence Lawyers in Delhi and Gurgaon

We handle anticipatory bail, regular bail, FIR quashing, criminal trials, cybercrime defence, economic offence representation, and matters involving bank fraud or cheating allegations. Urgent bail needs and early case assessment are prioritised.

In a criminal matter the first seventy-two hours usually decide the shape of everything that follows. Obtain the FIR, identify the sections invoked and whether the offence is bailable, and move the appropriate application before custody hardens into a pattern. Statements should not be given, and documents should not be handed over, without advice.

  • Anticipatory bail before the Sessions Court and High Court
  • Regular bail, interim bail and bail in economic offences
  • Quashing of FIRs and criminal proceedings
  • Defence in cheating, forgery and criminal breach of trust matters
  • Cyber fraud, online financial fraud and bank fraud allegations
  • Criminal trials, revisions and appeals

RERA & HRERA Complaint Lawyers in Gurgaon

We assist homebuyers and developers with RERA complaints, refund claims, possession delay cases, and builder–buyer disputes before HRERA and the consumer fora. We prepare evidence bundles and represent clients at hearings.

Where possession has been delayed beyond the date promised in the builder–buyer agreement, a buyer may generally seek either possession with interest for the delay, or withdrawal from the project with refund and interest. Which route is better depends on the stage of construction, the amount already paid and the buyer's own circumstances — that choice should be made before the complaint is drafted, not after.

Delhi High Court Advocates — Writs, Bail and Appeals

The firm appears before the Delhi High Court on the writ, criminal and appellate sides. Work here includes writ petitions against administrative and statutory action, bail applications where the sessions court has declined, quashing petitions, and first and second appeals from decrees of the district courts.

High Court practice is unforgiving about the record. An appeal is confined to what was pleaded and proved below, and a writ will not be entertained where a statutory remedy was available and not pursued. Where a matter is likely to reach the High Court, that should shape how the pleadings are drawn at the trial stage — which is why the firm prefers to be instructed early rather than after the ground has been lost.

  • Writ petitions under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution
  • Bail and anticipatory bail before the High Court
  • Quashing of FIRs and criminal proceedings
  • Criminal appeals and revisions
  • First and second appeals in civil matters
  • Applications for stay and interim protection

Questions & Answers

Common questions across the firm's practice. For anything specific to your matter, please arrange a consultation.

A mutual consent divorce ordinarily takes between six and eighteen months. The petition is filed jointly, a first motion is recorded, and a statutory cooling-off period of six months follows before the second motion — though courts may waive that period where the parties have already lived apart for a long time and the settlement is complete. A contested divorce is far longer, commonly two to five years or more in the Gurugram family courts, because it involves evidence, cross-examination and often parallel proceedings for maintenance or custody. The realistic timeline in any particular case depends on the grounds, the court's board and how far the parties are willing to settle.

You will generally need the child's birth certificate, school records and any medical records, proof of your own identity and address, evidence of your income and accommodation, and the marriage certificate. If matrimonial proceedings are already pending, bring copies of those petitions and any interim orders. Where the other parent's conduct is in issue, bring whatever documents that on: police complaints, messages, photographs or medical reports. Courts decide custody on the welfare of the child rather than the strength of either parent's claim, so material showing stability of schooling, care arrangements and the child's routine carries real weight.

An arrested person must be produced before a magistrate within twenty-four hours. In a bailable offence, bail is a matter of right and can be furnished at the police station or before the magistrate immediately. In a non-bailable offence, a bail application is moved before the magistrate or the sessions court and is usually taken up within a few days, depending on whether the investigating officer seeks custody and when the court lists it. Where arrest is apprehended but has not yet happened, an anticipatory bail application may be moved before the sessions court or the High Court. Speed matters: the application should be prepared the same day the FIR is known.

A complaint against a registered project is filed with the authority for the state in which the project is located — HRERA Gurugram for most of Gurugram. The complaint is filed in the prescribed form, setting out the parties, the project's registration number, the agreed date of possession and the relief claimed, whether that is possession with interest for delay, refund with interest, or compensation. It should be supported by the builder–buyer agreement, all payment receipts, demand letters and correspondence. The authority issues notice to the promoter, hears both sides and passes an order, which can then be executed for recovery if the promoter does not comply.

A demand notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act gives sixty days to discharge the liability. Within that period the borrower may make a representation or objection to the secured creditor, which the bank is required to consider and respond to with reasons. If the bank proceeds to take possession or other measures under Section 13(4), a securitisation application lies before the Debt Recovery Tribunal within forty-five days of that action. These periods are strict, and missing them removes the remedy rather than merely weakening it. Separately, a one-time settlement can often be negotiated in parallel. Bring the notice, the envelope, the loan account statement and the security documents.

Litigation places the dispute before a court, which hears both sides and passes a binding order. Mediation is a guided negotiation before a neutral mediator, where the parties arrive at the terms themselves; nothing is imposed and the discussion stays confidential. Mediation is usually faster, less expensive and less adversarial, and suits parties who must continue dealing with each other, such as co-parents. Where one side will not engage, or urgent protection or interim maintenance is needed, litigation is the right route. Courts in Delhi and Haryana frequently refer family matters to mediation first, and a settlement reached there can be recorded and made enforceable.

Verify the chain of title going back at least thirty years, confirm that the seller is the recorded owner and competent to sell, and inspect the mutation and revenue records. Check for existing mortgages, attachments, pending litigation or unpaid dues, and obtain an encumbrance certificate. Where the land is allotted or leasehold, confirm that transfer is permitted and that the authority's no-objection has been obtained. For constructed property, examine the sanctioned plan, occupation certificate and completion certificate. The agreement to sell should record possession, timelines and default consequences clearly, and the conveyance must be properly stamped and registered.

Duration depends on the forum, the relief sought and the conduct of the other side. Bail and interim applications are usually decided within days or weeks; a civil suit or contested matrimonial petition ordinarily runs into years; arbitration, mediation and RERA complaints are generally quicker. Costs consist of court charges and out-of-pocket expenses such as process service and certified copies. Terms are set according to the nature and stage of the matter and are confirmed before work begins.
